Bird migration along the Pacific Flyway

This project combines eBird abundance estimates, BirdFlow movement models, and NEXRAD weather radar to map when and where migratory birds move along the Pacific Flyway each spring, and how a changing climate is shifting those patterns.

Meet the six species

Each card shows the adult male, adult female, a juvenile, and the bird's range and migration path. Hover over the photos to hear the bird.

Interactive migration map

Explore modeled migration routes and seasonal abundance. Use the map's layer list and time controls to compare species and weeks.

Tonight's migration, live

BirdCast, from the Cornell Lab of Ornithology, maps bird migration across the U.S. in near real time using weather radar. Check it on spring and fall nights to see the Pacific Flyway in motion.

Water along the flyway

Streamflow from NOAA's National Water Model. Rivers, wetlands, and estuaries are critical stopover habitat where migrating birds rest and refuel, and climate-driven changes in water availability can reshape where they stop.

Methods

Three complementary data sources, each answering a different part of the migration question.

Where

eBird Status & Trends

Weekly relative-abundance estimates show where each species is concentrated across the season.

How

BirdFlow routes

Movement models built from eBird data simulate likely individual migration paths.

When

Weather radar

Nightly NEXRAD radar captures real migration pulses, showing the timing and intensity of movement.

Findings

Key results and figures from the analysis.

Data & tools

  • eBird Status & Trends (Cornell Lab of Ornithology)
  • BirdFlowR
  • NEXRAD weather radar, processed with bioRad and vol2birdR
  • NOAA National Water Model
  • BirdCast live migration maps (Cornell Lab of Ornithology)
  • Analysis in R (sf, terra, dplyr, ggplot2); maps in ArcGIS Online
